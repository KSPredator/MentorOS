# Chapter 1: Introduction to Foundation Models & AI Engineering

Topic: What is the primary shift from traditional ML Engineering to AI Engineering?
Explanation: The focus moves from training bespoke models from scratch to building applications on top of pre-trained foundation models.

Topic: What defines a "Foundation Model"?
Explanation: A large-scale model trained on vast amounts of unlabelled data that can be adapted for a wide range of downstream tasks.

Topic: What are the three layers of the AI Engineering Stack?
Explanation: The Model layer (foundation models), the Orchestration layer (prompting, tools, agents), and the Application layer (business logic and UI).

Topic: How do Large Language Models (LLMs) differ from earlier models?
Explanation: They scale massively in parameters and data, which unlocks emergent capabilities beyond simple pattern recognition.

Topic: What is the "Crawl, Walk, Run" framework for AI automation?
Explanation: Crawl (human involvement is mandatory), Walk (AI interacts directly with internal employees), Run (increased automation and direct AI interactions with external users).

Topic: What characterizes "Critical AI"?
Explanation: Use cases where high accuracy and reliability are mandatory, such as medical or financial tasks.

Topic: What characterizes "Complementary AI"?
Explanation: Use cases where users are more accepting of occasional mistakes because the AI acts as an assistant or brainstorming partner.

Topic: What is the difference between dynamic and static AI features?
Explanation: Static features use one model serving a group of users and update periodically; dynamic features require personalization or per-user models.

Topic: What is the lowest-effort technique to adapt a foundation model?
Explanation: Prompt Engineering.

Topic: What is the most resource-intensive technique to adapt a model?
Explanation: Fine-tuning, as it requires significant task-specific data and high compute effort.

Topic: What are the three levels of risk when adopting AI?
Explanation: Existential threat (competitors obsolete you), Missed opportunity (loss of productivity), and Strategic hedging (fear of being left behind).

Topic: Why is AI engineering different from full-stack engineering?
Explanation: AI engineering requires managing probabilistic systems, non-deterministic outputs, and continuous evaluation, whereas traditional software relies on deterministic logic.

# Chapter 2: Understanding Foundation Models

Topic: What is a "masked language model"?
Explanation: A model (like BERT) trained to predict a missing token within a sequence by looking at context both before and after the gap.

Topic: What is an "autoregressive language model"?
Explanation: A model trained to predict the next token given only the preceding tokens (e.g., GPT series).

Topic: What phase of training consumes the most compute resources?
Explanation: Pre-training, which often accounts for roughly 98% of total compute resources.

Topic: Who typically performs fine-tuning?
Explanation: Application developers tailoring a pre-trained model to specific application tasks.

Topic: What is the core architecture underlying modern LLMs?
Explanation: The Transformer architecture.

Topic: What are the two main components of a Transformer block?
Explanation: The Attention module and the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) module.

Topic: What is the primary advantage of the Attention mechanism?
Explanation: It allows the model to weigh the relevance of all other words in the input sequence when processing a specific token.

Topic: How does input processing differ from output generation in Transformers?
Explanation: Inputs can be processed in parallel, but outputs must be decoded sequentially.

Topic: What is an SSM?
Explanation: State Space Models, an alternative architecture aiming to handle longer context efficiently without the bottlenecks of standard attention.

Topic: What is Post-Training?
Explanation: Adjustments made after pre-training by model developers, such as instruction tuning or preference alignment.

Topic: Why is data quality crucial during pre-training?
Explanation: The model absorbs the facts, linguistic structures, and biases present in the foundational training data.

Topic: What is the fundamental cause of LLM inconsistency?
Explanation: They are probabilistic systems; the same input can yield slightly or drastically different outputs based on sampling dynamics.

Topic: What is sampling in model generation?
Explanation: The process of selecting the next token from the probability distribution generated by the model.

Topic: How can developers mitigate output inconsistency?
Explanation: By fixing sampling parameters, using memory structures, or caching input/output pairs.

Topic: What are structured outputs?
Explanation: Forcing the model to return data in a strict schema (like JSON) so it can be reliably parsed by deterministic code.

Topic: What is "Test Time Compute"?
Explanation: Strategies that give the model more time to process or generate intermediate steps before producing a final answer, improving reasoning.

Topic: What is a "token" in the context of LLMs?
Explanation: A sub-word unit of text; on average, a token is roughly 3/4 of a standard English word.

Topic: What is the role of preference finetuning?
Explanation: To align the model’s behavior with human preferences (e.g., being helpful and harmless) rather than just completing text.

# Chapter 3: Evaluation Methodology

Topic: Why is evaluating foundation models inherently difficult?
Explanation: Outputs are open-ended, and there is often no single "correct" answer, unlike traditional classification tasks.

Topic: What is "Exact Evaluation"?
Explanation: Checking for functional correctness, similar to unit tests—such as verifying if generated code compiles.

Topic: What is Cross Entropy?
Explanation: A mathematical metric measuring the difference between the model's predicted probability distribution and the actual dataset.

Topic: What is Perplexity?
Explanation: A measurement of how well a probability model predicts a sample; lower perplexity means the model is less "surprised" by the text.

Topic: When are similarity measurements used?
Explanation: For tasks where exact evaluation fails, output is compared against reference data using algorithmic similarity.

Topic: What is "AI as a Judge"?
Explanation: A technique where another, usually stronger, AI model evaluates the outputs based on a specific grading rubric.

Topic: What is a major limitation of "AI as a Judge"?
Explanation: It can be inconsistent, increases latency/costs, and lacks standardized scoring criteria (criteria ambiguity).

Topic: Why is human evaluation considered the gold standard?
Explanation: Because humans can intuitively grasp context, usefulness, and nuance better than automatic metrics.

Topic: What does "groundedness" measure?
Explanation: Whether the model's output is supported strictly by the provided context.

Topic: What is faithfulness in evaluation?
Explanation: Ensuring the model does not contradict the provided source material.

Topic: Why evaluate "harmfulness"?
Explanation: To prevent the model from generating toxic, biased, explicit, or malicious content.

Topic: What is the downside of continuous human evaluation?
Explanation: It is slow, expensive, and difficult to scale across rapid deployment cycles.

Topic: What is "spot-checking"?
Explanation: Randomly sampling a subset of model outputs for evaluation to monitor quality without evaluating every single generation.

Topic: What is criteria ambiguity?
Explanation: The challenge where evaluators interpret subjective guidelines (like "helpfulness") differently.

Topic: What do automatic metrics lack?
Explanation: They are fast but often fail to reflect real-world usefulness or semantic accuracy.

# Chapter 4: Evaluate AI Systems

Topic: What does TTFT stand for?
Explanation: Time to First Token, a critical latency metric for user experience.

Topic: What does TPOT stand for?
Explanation: Time per Output Token, measuring generation speed.

Topic: Why is TTFT critical for conversational bots?
Explanation: A fast TTFT signals to the user that the system is responsive, reducing perceived waiting time.

Topic: How is cost typically measured in LLM APIs?
Explanation: Cost per inference request, usually calculated per thousand or million tokens.

Topic: What is pass@1 in code generation?
Explanation: An evaluation metric that measures the probability that the very first code snippet generated passes all required tests.

Topic: What is the Elo score in model evaluation?
Explanation: An overall model quality metric adapted from chess, used to rank models based on head-to-head preference.

Topic: What factors influence the "Build Versus Buy" model decision?
Explanation: Data privacy, API cost versus engineering cost, copyright lineage, and model performance.

Topic: What is a major risk of relying on a commercial provider's API?
Explanation: Vendor risk—the provider may go out of business, deprecate models, or drastically change pricing.

Topic: What is Step 1 of designing an evaluation pipeline?
Explanation: Evaluate all components in the system individually (turn-based versus task-based).

Topic: What is Step 2 of designing an evaluation pipeline?
Explanation: Create an evaluation guideline that strictly defines criteria for what "good" looks like.

Topic: What is Step 3 of designing an evaluation pipeline?
Explanation: Define and select the evaluation methods and secure the evaluation dataset.

Topic: Why must evaluation pipelines track failures?
Explanation: To identify edge cases and implement fallback strategies when the AI system fails.

Topic: How do public benchmarks assist model selection?
Explanation: They provide a baseline comparison of how models handle domain-specific capabilities and instruction-following.

Topic: Why monitor latency and cost per task in production?
Explanation: Because these metrics heavily dictate the economic viability and user retention of the AI application.

Topic: What does consistency evaluation check?
Explanation: Whether the system delivers reliable outputs over time and across slightly different phrasing of the same task.

# Chapter 5: Prompt Engineering

Topic: What is Prompt Engineering?
Explanation: Crafting explicit instructions and examples to guide a model's output without altering its underlying weights.

Topic: What is Zero-Shot prompting?
Explanation: Asking the model to perform a task using in-context learning without providing any examples.

Topic: What is Few-Shot prompting?
Explanation: Providing a few examples of inputs and desired outputs within the prompt to guide the model's formatting and logic.

Topic: What is the difference between a System Prompt and a User Prompt?
Explanation: The system prompt sets the overarching behavior and rules, while the user prompt contains the specific immediate query.

Topic: Why should you write clear and explicit instructions?
Explanation: Because models are probabilistic; ambiguity leads to inconsistent guessing rather than strict adherence.

Topic: How do you handle complex tasks in prompts?
Explanation: Break the complex task down into simpler, sequential subtasks.

Topic: What does "Give the Model Time to Think" mean?
Explanation: Prompting techniques (like Chain of Thought) that require the model to output reasoning steps before declaring an answer.

Topic: Why does iteration matter in prompt engineering?
Explanation: Initial prompts rarely cover all edge cases; testing against real examples highlights areas where instructions must be refined.

Topic: What is Prompt Extraction?
Explanation: A defensive security concern where users attempt to trick the model into revealing its proprietary system prompt.

Topic: What is Jailbreaking or Prompt Injection?
Explanation: Security vulnerabilities where users input malicious text designed to override the system instructions.

Topic: What is Defensive Prompting?
Explanation: Crafting prompts specifically designed to recognize and refuse prompt injection or extraction attempts.

Topic: What is Context Efficiency?
Explanation: Optimizing the information packed into a prompt to minimize token usage, which reduces cost and latency.

Topic: What is the standard template structure for a robust prompt?
Explanation: Task + context + desired style or tone.

Topic: Why is prompt engineering considered low effort?
Explanation: It requires minimal data and no expensive model training compute to test hypotheses quickly.

Topic: How is information extraction handled via prompts?
Explanation: By providing strict schemas and few-shot examples to force the model to output structured data from unstructured text.

# Chapter 6: RAG and Agents

Topic: What does RAG stand for?
Explanation: Retrieval-Augmented Generation.

Topic: What is the primary purpose of RAG?
Explanation: To connect a foundation model to an external, up-to-date knowledge base to reduce hallucinations and provide proprietary context.

Topic: What is an Embedding?
Explanation: A numerical vector representation of text that captures semantic meaning.

Topic: What is the role of Vector Databases in RAG?
Explanation: They store embeddings and allow rapid mathematical similarity searches to find relevant documents for the prompt.

Topic: What is "chunking" in RAG architectures?
Explanation: Breaking large documents into smaller text segments before generating embeddings, ensuring retrieved context is dense and relevant.

Topic: Why is RAG considered "moderate effort"?
Explanation: It requires building retrieval pipelines and data infrastructure, which is more complex than prompting but less intensive than finetuning.

Topic: What differentiates an AI Agent from a standard RAG chatbot?
Explanation: Agents can orchestrate multi-step workflows, call tools, and make autonomous decisions to achieve an objective.

Topic: What is Tool Calling?
Explanation: Providing the model with a defined list of external functions (APIs) it can choose to execute.

Topic: Why is the Orchestration Layer crucial for agents?
Explanation: It manages the routing between prompts, tools, memory, and the foundation model.

Topic: What is a major risk in autonomous agents?
Explanation: Compound errors; a hallucinated tool output can cause the agent to loop endlessly or execute destructive actions.

Topic: What is conversational memory in agents?
Explanation: Systems built to pass past interactions back into the stateless model so it "remembers" the context of the workflow.

Topic: How does RAG solve the model obsolescence problem?
Explanation: It allows the system to access real-time or new data without requiring expensive retraining of the foundation model weights.

Topic: What is query rewriting in RAG?
Explanation: An agentic pattern where the LLM reformulates the user's initial prompt into a more effective search query before retrieving documents.

Topic: What is the difference between extraction and aggregation RAG?
Explanation: Extraction pulls specific facts; aggregation requires summarizing broad themes across multiple retrieved chunks.

Topic: Why are low-code/no-code platforms adopting agents?
Explanation: To allow non-technical builders to connect foundation models to internal tools through graphical orchestration layers.

# Chapter 7 & 8: Finetuning and Dataset Engineering

Topic: What is the primary goal of Finetuning?
Explanation: To further train a foundation model on task-specific data to alter its innate behavior, tone, or format adherence.

Topic: Why is finetuning considered "significant effort"?
Explanation: It requires curating large, high-quality datasets and substantial compute resources.

Topic: What is Dataset Engineering?
Explanation: The meticulous process of cleaning, structuring, deduplicating, and curating data specifically for model training.

Topic: Why is deduplication important in datasets?
Explanation: If duplicate records are over-represented, the model memorizes them rather than learning generalized logic.

Topic: What is the danger of toxic or biased data during finetuning?
Explanation: The model will reliably mimic the toxicity and biases present in the finetuning dataset.

Topic: What are annotations in dataset engineering?
Explanation: Human or AI-generated labels attached to data to guide supervised finetuning.

Topic: What is the difference between RAG and Finetuning?
Explanation: RAG injects knowledge via the prompt at runtime; Finetuning alters the model's neural weights during a training phase.

Topic: Can you finetune models via commercial APIs?
Explanation: Yes, some commercial providers offer finetuning APIs, but open-source models offer more control over the weights.

Topic: What is Supervised Finetuning (SFT)?
Explanation: Providing the model with high-quality input-output pairs so it learns to follow instructions accurately.

Topic: Why is data diversity necessary?
Explanation: A monolithic dataset causes catastrophic forgetting, where the model loses its general capabilities in favor of the narrow task.

Topic: What is the impact of prompt engineering vs finetuning on latency?
Explanation: Finetuning allows shorter prompts (saving TTFT and cost) because the instructions are baked into the weights.

Topic: What is domain-specific capability?
Explanation: Finetuning a model specifically to excel in niche fields (like legal or medical terminology).

Topic: How does data volume scale from pre-training to finetuning?
Explanation: Pre-training uses trillions of tokens; finetuning often requires only thousands of high-quality examples.

Topic: What is preference alignment?
Explanation: A post-training technique (often using human feedback) to teach the finetuned model which valid response format is actually preferred.

Topic: What does dataset engineering overlap with?
Explanation: Data management infrastructure and evaluation benchmarks.

# Chapter 9 & 10: Inference Optimization, Architecture & Feedback

Topic: What is Inference in AI?
Explanation: The production phase where a deployed model processes prompts and generates outputs.

Topic: Why is Inference Optimization critical?
Explanation: LLM inference is highly compute-bound and memory-bound; optimization reduces latency and operating costs.

Topic: What is Batching?
Explanation: Processing multiple independent user requests simultaneously on the GPU to maximize hardware utilization.

Topic: What is Decoding in inference?
Explanation: The sequential generation of output tokens one by one based on probability distributions.

Topic: Why is memory bandwidth often the bottleneck during inference?
Explanation: Because generating each token requires loading massive model weights from GPU memory to the compute cores.

Topic: What is the purpose of the Infrastructure layer in AI architecture?
Explanation: Managing compute resources, data serving, and monitoring model health.

Topic: Why is User Feedback essential in AI Engineering?
Explanation: Because probabilistic models drift or fail in unpredictable ways; feedback creates loops for continuous evaluation.

Topic: What is explicit user feedback?
Explanation: Direct signals from users, such as thumbs up/down, corrections, or written reviews on the AI's output.

Topic: What is implicit user feedback?
Explanation: Behavioral metrics, such as a user accepting an auto-complete suggestion, copying text, or abandoning the interaction.

Topic: How does architecture separate model layers from application layers?
Explanation: It allows non-technical leaders and engineers to swap underlying foundation models without rewriting business logic.

Topic: Why build fallback strategies into the architecture?
Explanation: To ensure the application remains functional (using smaller models or graceful errors) if a primary API provider fails.

Topic: What is the role of an AI interface?
Explanation: To bridge the gap between the orchestration layer and the end user efficiently.

Topic: How does AI engineering handle regulatory risks?
Explanation: Architecture must account for IP laws, data privacy, and data lineage when serving outputs to users.

Topic: What is a "Fatal" maintenance risk in AI architecture?
Explanation: Legal liability caused by a model trained on copyrighted data or sudden regulatory bans.

Topic: Why track edge cases in production?
Explanation: Production traffic exposes the system to edge cases missed in the Golden Dataset, guiding future prompt adjustments.

Topic: What is the overarching goal of AI Engineering?
Explanation: To build robust, reliable, and measurable systems that extract real business value from unpredictable foundation models.
